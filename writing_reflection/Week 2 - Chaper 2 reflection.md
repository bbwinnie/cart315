Reading this chapter gave me many new ideas that i had never really thought about before. One thing that interested me most is that one game can have many different versions, and the rules of a game do not always have to stay the same. In Arvi Teikari's BABA IS YOU, for example, you are not only a player, but you also feel a little like a game designer. You play the game, but at the same time, you can change some of its rules. This gives players a lot of freedom. 
This idea reminded me of THE LEGEND OF ZELDA:ECHOES OF WISDOM, which I have been playing recently. The character cannot jump very high because the game has a limit for jumping. However, players can copy different objects in the game and use them to build stairs or platforms to reach higher places. I think every player may have their own favorite object or method to do this. The basic rules are still there, but players can decide how to use them in different ways.
I also liked Pippin Barr's idea that game rules can be designed, changed, and tested. When he created different versions of PONGS, changing one small rule sometimes created a completely different game. I also found the idea of "having a conversation with the rules" very interesting. During the design process, you may discover a new idea or even find a bug. However, a bug is not always a bad thing. Sometimes an unexpected result can create a new possibility or even lead to a better game rule. 
For me, chapter 2 shows that game rules are not fixed. They can change in many ways and can create new ideas during both game design and play.
